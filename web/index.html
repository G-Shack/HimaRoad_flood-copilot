About the system

What it measured, and how to read it.

You give an area and a flood date. The system compares Sentinel-1 radar images from before and after the event, and adds Sentinel-2 when the sky is clear. It overlays OpenStreetMap from before the event and finds settlements that can lose the road connection to the nearest town or hospital in four hypothetical scenarios.

Measured results

Case study, Trishuli, 26 Aug 2026. 22 of 73 settlements potentially road-cut in the primary scenario. 102 potentially exposed mapped buildings. 121 suspected affected road links.

Flood segmentation model (UNet), development events. Precision 0.8008, recall 0.9457, IoU 0.7656 (rule baseline IoU 0.6096).

Copilot. 62,263 evidence claims from the Trishuli run. Every answer cites its claims. A one-page report in English or Nepali takes about five seconds.

How to read the evidence

Overlap with a hazard means potentially exposed. Operational road status stays unknown until a field team reports it.

Areas that radar cannot see (layover and shadow in steep valleys) stay unknown, never zero.

Sentinel satellites revisit an area every few days. Satellite time is not event time.

The four access scenarios are hypothetical. They show which settlements depend on which links.
